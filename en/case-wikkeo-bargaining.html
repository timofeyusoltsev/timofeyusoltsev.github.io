Price negotiation: more deals, busier chats

The platform allowed negotiation, but buyers did not know it. I built it into the order flow as an explicit action,
not a guess.

Role
Lead Designer
Timeline
2025 — 2026
Platforms
Web, Mobile
Results
7,000 sales in a month
Research

The demand was there,
nobody knew about it

I surveyed buyers and sellers across the platform’s community groups. 18 of 24 buyers said it had never occurred to them that you could negotiate here, but they were willing to try. To test behavior rather than a stated intent, I asked them to message a few sellers and ask for a discount:
7 of the 10 who tried reached a deal, and they liked the experience.

Sellers met the idea warily. That changed once it was clear the discount is theirs to set, and the mechanic works for sales volume rather than against margin.

Competitive analysis

Everywhere negotiation lives in the chat — and gets lost there

On Avito there is direct contact with the seller, and the most persistent buyers do agree on a discount, but there is no mechanic as such: negotiation exists only inside the chat and depends on whether the buyer thought to write at all.

On China’s 1688 the flow is carried through: the buyer messages the seller about an item, they discuss the price, the seller agrees and sets a new amount, and the buyer gets a notification that they can buy at the new price. The result of the deal lands in the item instead of staying as words in a chat.

What both share is that the entrance to negotiation is hidden inside the chat. That set where to look for a solution: leave the deal itself in the chat, and move the entrance to where the buyer already is.

Prioritization

Hints inside the chat would not have been enough

The obvious solution — hint in the chat that you can negotiate — broke on arithmetic: conversion into chat was low to begin with, and the hint would only reach people who had already got there. The entrance had to sit before the chat.

  • ✓ An explicit entrance in the listing and the order A badge and a button onboard both sides right inside the purchase flow. Cheap and fits an MVP.
  • — Hints inside the chat Conversion into chat is too small — the mechanic would go unnoticed.
  • — A discount slider on the listing It overloads the listing. The idea of showing the average sale price is worth returning to later.
  • — Personalized notifications that the seller will cut the price This belongs to motivation and upsell, not to negotiation itself. It was taken into work as a separate task.
  • — A seller rating based on negotiation success The mechanic is not obvious to the buyer and sits closer to seller trust.
Solution

Negotiation lives in the order, not in the chat

01

The seller turns negotiation on

In the dashboard the seller marks their items as open to offers. Taking part is voluntary — that removed the main objection raised during research.

02

The buyer places an order and asks for a discount

The order is created, the price is discussed in the chat, but the result of the deal lands in the order itself: the buyer sees the new price and pays it.

03

The discount is spread across items

The seller sets one discount for the whole order, and the system spreads it proportionally across the line items. That solved partial refunds in advance.

Results

Negotiation became a mass behavior within the first month

5% → 15%+conversion into chat with the seller
7 000sales through negotiation in the first month

The average check dipped for a while but was offset by sales volume — an expected trade-off, accepted in advance. The side effect turned out to matter more: seller chats became several times busier, and with them the number of buyers following sellers grew. The flip side is that sellers stopped keeping up with replies, and chat load became the next problem to solve.
