PREMIER · 20M+ MAU

One subscription
across three services

I merged the PREMIER, RUTUBE and Gazprom Bonus subscriptions into one product: one profile, one payment flow,
with the whole value visible before paying.

Context

An ecosystem on paper,
three products in the interface

PREMIER gave access to its own series and films, Rutube to video hosting, Gazprom Bonus to its ecosystem. On paper this was one ecosystem bundle, but the user ran into three different products: different profiles, different payment flows, different language describing the same value.
The benefit had to be assembled in your own head.

Hypothesis

What slows the decision to pay is not price, it is confusion

The business hypothesis we entered the project with: if the user has one profile, one clear payment flow and the whole value of the subscription visible at once, they decide to pay faster, understand better what they are paying for, and feel disappointed less often after the purchase.

That gave a set of testable metrics: purchase conversion stood for the speed of the decision, average check for understanding of value, churn for the absence of disappointment after paying.

Alternatives

The market knew how to retain with price — the job was to learn how to explain

Ready-made models for bundling subscriptions existed on the market, and almost all of them were cheaper to build. But the hypothesis said the decision to pay is slowed by confusion, not price — and that ruled most of them out.

  • ✓ One profile and one payment flow The only option that removes the cause instead of announcing it. The market later arrived at the same model: Disney announced folding Hulu fully into Disney+ on one platform — for engagement and lower churn.
  • — A price bundle: a discount on three subscriptions, three separate entrances That is how Disney+ / Hulu / HBO Max is put together: one bill, but three apps and three accounts. The model works — 3-month retention for the bundle is 73% against 54–56% for the same services separately. What retains here is price, not understanding, so this option cannot test our hypothesis.
  • — An aggregator model, like Prime Video Channels Partner subscriptions connect inside one interface and one bill, but stay separate lines in billing, and some content sends you off into the partner’s app. For us that would have kept three payment flows — exactly the thing the project existed to remove.
  • — Lean on a flagship brand instead of explaining the value Ecosystem subscriptions in Russia have an anchor: 43% of people with several subscriptions name Yandex Plus as their main one. Gazprom Bonus holds 2% of the market. There was no recognition to carry the bundle, so the value had to be shown by the interface.
  • — Keep growing the bundle instead of simplifying the entrance Half the subscribers of ecosystem services never use the streaming services included: dormant services pile up, and one day the whole subscription gets cancelled. Adding services to a bundle nobody understands means growing future churn.
Solution

One purchase flow instead of three parallel ones

01

One profile

Entitlements for all three services are tied to a single account. The user no longer has to work out where one product ends and another begins.

02

Value before payment

What is in the bundle and what it costs are brought into one comparison, in the same units. Comparing plans stopped being the user’s job.

03

One payment flow

Choosing a plan, the payment method and confirmation — a linear path with no jumps between services and no second sign-in.

Results

The subscription began to read as one ecosystem with a clear benefit

+14%purchase conversion of the unified subscription
+21%average check
−11%monthly churn
+21%LTV over the first 3 months

The hypothesis held on all three metrics: the path to payment got shorter and clearer, and the fragmentation of experience between services went down. The project laid the ground for further LTV and retention growth — one profile and one payment flow became the base on which ecosystem offers can be built.
