Offline mode on mobile

For a sizeable part of the audience, watching without a connection is not an edge case but a basic expectation. I built the whole flow: online → downloaded → offline → watching.

Role
Product Designer
Timeline
2025
Platforms
iOS, Android
Results
+8% retention
Audience

Who needs this, and why
it is not a secondary feature

The segments the feature was built for: people on the move — commuting and travelling; people with unstable internet; heavy mobile series watchers. For them offline mode is not an extra option but a basic expectation of a video service.

That changed the priority of the task: this was not about the convenience of a minority, but about whether the service works in one of its main modes of use.

Problem

Downloading worked in silence

Downloading an episode was possible, but the state of the process stayed opaque: whether the download was running or stalled, whether there was room on the device, what was already available offline and what was not.
Errors arrived with no reason given.

As a result the feature was used less than demand allowed — not because the need was missing, but because there was no trust in the result.

Alternatives

The industry automated downloads — we first had to explain them

Offline mechanics in video services are mature and well documented. Almost all of them build on top of a process the user already trusts — and distrust was exactly our problem.

  • ✓ The whole flow made transparent: states, storage, behavior offline What went into build was predictability of the existing mechanism, not new capabilities on top of it.
  • — Auto-downloading the next episodes, like Netflix Smart Downloads Netflix deletes a watched episode and pulls the next one in advance, working on Wi-Fi only to save data. The mechanic is smart, but it requires trusting the automation. Our user did not even understand what was happening with an ordinary download: automating an opaque process would amplify the problem.
  • — Offline as a privilege of a higher plan Netflix does not offer Smart Downloads on the ad-supported plan — monetizing the feature exists and works. But it assumes the basic flow is already reliable. Selling access to something people do not trust is premature.
  • — Leave download expiry in the dark The DRM industry standard: an availability window of up to 30 days, a viewing window of 48 hours from first play, plus a cap on downloads per device. Technically it is a given, but as product behavior it is the source of the worst case: downloaded it and ran out of time. So the deadlines have to be shown as a timer, not discovered after the fact.
  • — A downloads list with no device storage management In Netflix downloads you can see the size of each file, used and free space, sorting, and a warning when storage runs low. A section without that data stays just a list — it does not answer “what do I delete to make this fit”.
Solution

The whole flow,
not just a download button

01

Transparent download states

Queued, in progress, paused, failed — every state is shown and explained, including the reason a download is not moving.

02

Managing device storage

How much the downloads take, what can be deleted and how much that frees — before storage runs out, not after.

03

Predictable behavior offline

With no connection it is clear what can be watched right now and what needs to be online. The app stopped looking broken when there is no signal.

Results

Offline viewing became a stable pattern of use

+8%30-day retention
−10%churn
+9%engagement by episodes watched
+6%DAU of the mobile apps

The feature moved a set of connected metrics rather than one: both watch depth and how often people came back to the app went up. That confirms the original frame — offline was not a convenience but a condition of using the service for a noticeable part of the audience.
